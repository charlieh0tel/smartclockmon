Three receivers, one antenna, one half-deaf Z3805A

An HP 58503A, an HP Z3805A and a u-blox NEO-M8T share one GPS antenna through a distribution splitter. Every minute the SmartClocks' satellite tables were paired with the u-blox's sky report, satellite by satellite, to see which signals each SmartClock can hold.

24 hours, 1440 paired minutes, 2026-10-03 00:02 to 2026-10-04 00:01 UTC: a full day of the ionosphere and a sidereal day of satellite geometry.

Elevation mask: 10° on both SmartClocks. Satellites below 10° are left out of every count, the u-blox's included.

Reading it

  • The 58503A holds nearly every satellite from 39 dB-Hz up, 88% at 36 to 39, and half at 30 to 33.
  • The Z3805A holds nothing below 39 dB-Hz, 37% at 39 to 42, and 92% from 42 to 45. Its threshold sits at least 7 dB above the 58503A's.
  • It does that with 20 dB of low-noise gain ahead of it that the others lack. That stage masks the Z3805A's own noise figure and any loss ahead of its front end, so the C/N reaching its engine is at least what the u-blox reports, likely more. The 7 dB is a lower bound on its shortfall.
  • The amplifier's own effect has not been isolated. On 2026-09-23, before it was added, the Z3805A was already weak: 1 to 2 satellites on average in its locked hours, six only briefly, with the same average signal reading, 27.6, that it shows now. Its later recovery to 2 to 4 satellites came after a master_reset, a power cycle and the amplifier together.
  • A separate 12-hour run on 2026-09-30, Z3805A against the u-blox alone, gave the same picture: 2.7 satellites a minute against 9.0.

These are inferences from the counts, not measurements of the Z3805A's RF path. Moving its engine into the 58503A, or the 58503A's into it, would show whether the loss is in the engine or in the unit around it. The B1 engine's lack of the B4's enhanced filtering leaves out-of-band overload as a possibility that an L1 band-pass filter would test.

Method

  • Every 60 s, each SmartClock's status screen was read through smartclock-web, the same read its status page makes, and paired with the newest SKY report from gpsd on apu-0 (under a second old).
  • Both SmartClocks were logged over the same hours. A satellite counts as held when the SmartClock lists it as tracked in that minute's screen.
  • Only GPS satellites the u-blox marked as used are counted, so every satellite in the tables was one a healthy receiver could use.
  • Satellites below 10° are left out of every count, the u-blox's included. Both SmartClocks' elevation masks read 10° (:GPS:SATellite:TRACking:EMANgle?), so they are told to ignore anything lower.
