SmartClock Disciplining Loop

How the receiver steers its 10811 oscillator to GPS, as read from the firmware images: a proportional–integral loop on the ten-second mean of the phase error, with the oscillator's own aging curve fed forward into the integrator. Every constant below is one the code contains.

The loop

Once a second the firmware reads one interval from the FPGA counter. Every tenth second it takes the mean x̄ of the ten readings — the number :SYNChronization:TINTerval? reports — and runs one update:

e = x̄ − x₀x₀ is a setpoint, normally 0f ← (1 − a)·f + a·ea = 29.75 / τd = clamp(b + c / y, −M, +M)y = t / 2700 s, from the aging fitI ← I + 10·k·f + 10·d / 2700k = 1 / (4·G·τ²)u = K·f + B + I + c₀·sK = 1 / (G·τ)

u is the EFC word sent to the DAC. B is the EFC that was in force when the loop closed, less c₀·s, so closing the loop does not move the oscillator; s is the health monitor's exponential average of the oscillator's oven current (0.1 of each fresh reading, 0.9 of the last), and c₀ is the value :DIAGnostic:ROSCillator:TCOefficient reads and writes — a calibration constant in the EEPROM block, in EFC units per unit of the current channel, applied at every update and adjusted by nothing but its own setter. That term tracks the oven rather than the phase; the console word xcal measures it by fitting EFC against current.

What kind of loop that is

Take the oscillator as an integrator: an EFC change of Δu shifts its fractional frequency by G·Δu, and frequency integrates into phase. With the proportional gain K = 1/(Gτ) and an integral gain of k per second, and leaving the prefilter out, the closed loop is second order:

s² + (G·K)·s + G·k = s² + s/τ + 1/(4τ²) = (s + 1/(2τ))²

A double real pole at −1/(2τ), damping ratio exactly 1: that is what the gains are chosen to give. The prefilter moves it.

The prefilter on the error is a first-order low-pass at p = 2.975/τ (time constant τ / 2.975, about 168 s at τ = 500 s), and it sits inside the loop, ahead of both gains. The characteristic equation becomes s²(s + p) + p·(s/τ + 1/(4τ²)) = 0, a cubic. For the 10 s update itself its roots are a real pole at −0.37/τ and a pair at (−1.35 ± 0.51j)/τ, damping ratio 0.94 (the s-plane below).

The slow real pole sets the tail: about 2.7τ, 1360 s at τ = 500 s, 1900 s at 700 s, 2710 s at 1000 s. Started from a phase error, the loop removes 63 % of it in about τ, overshoots by about 20 % near 3τ, and settles back from the other side at the slow pole's rate.

The update is discrete at 10 s; the factor 10 on the integrator term is the sample period. The poles above are the discrete update's, mapped to the s-plane; the continuous cubic gives nearly the same, −0.37/τ and (−1.30 ± 0.56j)/τ.

G cancels out of the closed-loop dynamics: it only scales the gains so the poles land where they do whatever the oscillator's actual sensitivity. If the real oscillator gain differs from the assumed G, the loop gain is off by that ratio and the poles move.

The aging feed-forward

The dashed path is what makes the receiver's holdover good. While the loop is closed, pllp averages the current-compensated EFC, u − c₀·s, over each 2700 s (45 min) window and pushes one sample into a ring of 64 — 48 hours. The curv task fits

EFC(t) = a + b·y + c·ln yy = t / 2700 s, t the seconds since cold start

to those samples (a least-squares line, plus a one-dimensional search on c once there are enough points). The slope of that curve now, b + c/y, is d: the EFC change per 2700 s the fit predicts. Each update the integrator advances by 10·d/2700 — the predicted change over ten seconds — before the loop sees any phase error from it. In holdover the same curve keeps advancing the EFC with nothing to correct it; startup_pll also takes its first EFC from it after a restart.

The Z3815A and 58503B compute the same slope once per fit, not every ten seconds, and each update moves a running value toward it with time constant τ, so a new fit's change of slope reaches the oscillator over about τ rather than at once.

Samples are weighted by how much of the window was averaged: 3 if all 2700 seconds, 2 if at least 675, else 1; and everything in the first three hours after a cold start gets weight 1. A fit needs at least three samples, so d is zero for the first hours after a power-up.

Starting up

Before pll_normal, startup_pll runs the same update law with its own time constant, recomputing K, k and a from it every update. It first waits for sixteen consecutive ten-second means within ±150 ns, then lengthens its constant from 150 s by 5 s per update until it is within 5 s of τ, sets it to τ, and hands over. The Z3801A's firmware does the same; the 58503B starts from :LTIMe:INIT instead of a fixed 150 s.
